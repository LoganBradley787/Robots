## Bugs: simulation core

### S1. A robot rebuilt on two ticks in a row ignores every force but gravity

- Severity: high for big robots and big fights, low for a small duel. Status: Reproduced once. Size: M.
- Where: `packages/sim-core/src/world/World.ts:1085` (`motion`), the `lastKicks` branch at lines 1091 to 1092.
- Cause: a rebuild replaces a robot's bodies and hands the new ones their velocity as a kick on the next step. For a body kicked on this step, `motion()` answers "kick velocity plus one step of gravity" instead of reading the body. That is right for free fall. It discards whatever else acted during that step: thrust, wheel drive, ground contact, drag, a rope.
- Scenario: a slab with six thrusters at full throttle, zero gravity, 31 ticks. With no part loss it reaches 12.6 m/s. Losing one frame on every tick it reaches 0.47 m/s, one tick's worth of thrust. `rebuild.ts:38` notes that a large robot under fire loses a part on most ticks.
- Change: for a piece with a single body, set the velocity on the body when it is created, so no kick and no special case is needed. Keep the kick only for jointed pieces, and there add the velocity change Rapier reported over the step instead of assuming gravity alone.

### S2. The tick after any part loss, scripts and sensors read the robot's velocity as zero

- Severity: medium. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `World.ts:2074` to `2077` (`fillFeed`), and the contact velocity in `readReference`. Both read the physics state directly, which reports a new body at rest until its kick has been stepped.
- Scenario: a flyer moving at (20, 5) m/s loses one frame. On the next tick its own script reads `self.vel` as (0, 0) and `self.angVel` as 0, and a radar watching it reports a contact velocity of (0, 0). One tick later both are right again.
- Effect: a hover script that damps on `self.vel.y` glitches on every hit. A turret that leads by `contact.vel` aims at a standing target for a tick each time its target loses a part, which is exactly when it is hitting.
- Change: one `velocityOf(body)` helper that prefers the pending or last kick, used by `fillFeed`, `readReference`, and the muzzle velocity a shell inherits.

### S3. A fast-spinning robot's wheels and turrets take crash damage with nothing touching them

- Severity: medium. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S to M.
- Where: `World.ts:814` (`checkImpacts`). The hit is each body's own velocity change in one step. A wheel, or anything on a rotator or piston, is its own body. When the robot spins, that body's velocity changes by `w * w * r * dt` each step with no collision. Past the default safe speed of 12 m/s (`world/crash.ts:9`) that counts as a crash.
- Scenario: a 9 wide car with two heavy gyros, zero gravity, holding the spin key. At about 13 rad/s both wheels (4.1 m from the center) are destroyed.
- Change: measure the hit on the robot, not on the link. Use the robot's mass-weighted velocity change for any body that had no contact of its own this step.

### S4. Joint motors are tuned from the carried side only

- Severity: medium to high. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `behaviors/rotator.ts:25` to `41` (gains from the turned body's inertia only), `behaviors/piston.ts:31` to `41` (from the carried mass only).
- Scenario: a joint whose mount side is one frame and whose carried side is an 8 wide hull. The rotator's frame is still spinning at 3 to 4 rad/s nine seconds after the key is released. The piston throws the 1 kg frame at over 24 m/s in one step, which the crash check then destroys. The validator reports nothing. The same state appears by itself when a hull is shot down to a stub under its turret.
- Change: size the gains from the reduced inertia `Ic * Ip / (Ic + Ip)` (and reduced mass for the piston). A heavy turret on a heavy hull is unchanged.

### S5. A blast gives a NaN push to a part exactly `lift` below its center

- Severity: high if it reaches the physics step. Status: Confirmed in the blast function. Its effect on a whole world was not tested. Size: S.
- Where: `packages/sim-core/src/damage/explosion.ts:68` to `70`. The push direction is `(dx, dy + lift)` divided by its length, and the length is not checked.
- Recipe: `blastEffects({ x: 0, y: 0 }, { radius: 2.5, damage: 80, pushRadius: 4, push: 25, lift: 1 }, [{ x: 0, y: -1, angle: 0 }], [])` returns a push of `{ jx: NaN, jy: NaN }`. Those are a laser's numbers, and one cell straight below is where a laser's mount usually sits. Warheads and charges use lift 1.5, lasers, lances, and cannons lift 1.
- It needs the robot exactly level, so it is rare in play but real for a robot that has not yet turned (a fresh spawn, a straight fall).
- Change: guard the length and pick a fixed direction at zero. The push also reverses completely across that point (about 19 N s straight down just below it, 19 N s straight up just above it, with the numbers in the recipe), so a smooth blend toward "up" would be better than a guard alone.

## Bugs: weapons

### W1. A charged gun's recoil counts as a crash on the robot that fired it

- Severity: high. Status: Confirmed. Size: S for the simple fix.
- Where: `World.ts:1395` (recoil) and `World.ts:1386` (backfire) push with `quiet: true`. At `World.ts:1166`, quiet means "do not mark the bodies unsettled", and unsettled bodies are the only ones `checkImpacts` skips (`World.ts:823`). So a quiet push is the one push the crash check does see. That was right for the gun (2 N s). The cannon is 800 N s and the lance 300.
- `docs/design/02-parts-and-blueprints.md` and the comment above `runCannons` both say the recoil is quiet so that no fuze goes off.
- Recipe:
  ```json
  {
    "format": 1,
    "name": "selfkill",
    "grid": ["C Cn> = = =", ". = = = ="],
    "bindings": [{ "key": "f", "target": "cannon", "channel": "fire", "value": 1, "mode": "hold" }]
  }
  ```
  `pnpm sim run selfkill.json --seconds 7.6 --y 400 --unlimited --keys "f:0-7"`. The robot (42 kg) is in free fall with nothing near it. Output: `t= 7.00 cannon@1,1 fired`, `t= 7.02 lost 1 part: cannon@1,1`, `t= 7.02 explosion`, `t= 7.02 lost its core with every other part`.
- Thresholds from the numbers: crash damage starts when recoil over mass passes 12 m/s, so under about 67 kg for a cannon and 25 kg for a lance. A warhead's fuze (5 m/s) trips under 160 kg for a cannon and 60 kg for a lance, so a cannon and an armed missile rack on one light drone would set each other off.
- The stock robots sit clear of it by mass: `enemy-cannon-drone` is 118 kg and `enemy-lance-drone` 86 kg. A light scout with a lance walks straight into it, and neither `validate` nor the playbook warns.
- The bolt's push on its target (`World.ts:1456`) goes through the same path, so a light robot that takes a bolt probably takes crash damage on top. Not run.
- Change: make recoil and backfire pushes exempt from the crash check (the simple version: not quiet, so the robot is unsettled for two ticks). Then decide on purpose whether a bolt's shove may crash a light target.

### W2. A wall's shadow on a wide bolt's edge ends about 10 m behind the wall

- Severity: medium. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `World.ts:1426` to `1431`. Each tick, an edge line of a wide bolt stops at the first terrain on that tick's ray, and nothing is remembered between ticks. The test at `packages/sim-core/test/cannon.test.ts:435` passes because the ray starts 10 m behind the bolt, so the wall is still on it for 10 m.
- Scenario: the same test with the hidden frame moved back. At 10 m behind the wall it survives. At 15, 20, 25, and 30 m it is destroyed.
- Change: remember which edge lines have met terrain ahead of the bolt (a small bitmask on the bolt, hashed) and keep them dead for the rest of the flight.

## Bugs: builder and blueprint editing

### E1. Rotating a multi-cell part onto another part silently deletes one of them on save

- Severity: high. Status: Reproduced once. Size: M.
- Where: `packages/sim-core/src/blueprint/edit.ts:87` (`setPartRotation` does not check the new footprint), `blueprint/toGrid.ts:48` (an occupied cell is overwritten).
- Scenario: grid `['. . = .', '. F Lz^ F', 'C F F F']`. Rotate the laser to 90 degrees so its barrel covers a frame. The draft still has 7 parts. The saved file has 6, with no validation issue, and reopening shows 6.
- Change: make grid serialization refuse overlapping cells, and refuse or show an overlapping rotation in the builder. Fixing the serializer also covers resized bays.

### E2. Save As can give a secondary core's script and a recipe's script the same file

- Severity: high. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `blueprint/scripts.ts:118`. The recursive call for a recipe starts a new set of used file names instead of sharing the parent's.
- Scenario: a second core with scope `child` and a script `guide`, plus a recipe named `child` with its own `guide`. Both are filed as `<name>.child.guide.js`. The second write replaces the first, and the saved blueprint then fails validation for a duplicate file.
- Change: share one allocator across the blueprint and its recipes, and check for collisions before any file is written.

### E3. Erasing the primary core strands the next core's controls

- Severity: medium. Status: Reproduced once. Size: M.
- Where: `blueprint/edit.ts:54` to `59`, against the rule at `blueprint/validate.ts:177`.
- Scenario: grid `C C`, with backup controls listed under the second core. Erase the first. The second becomes primary but keeps its secondary entry, which the validator rejects, so the robot cannot be deployed.
- Change: when a core is promoted, move its controls to the top level and drop its secondary entry.

### E4. Box selection misses the barrel or wall of a multi-cell part

- Severity: low. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `packages/app/src/builder/editorState.ts:114` tests part origins only. A click, at line 107, uses the whole footprint.
- Change: select a part when any cell of its footprint is in the box.

### E5. Flipping a blueprint whose root part is multi-cell moves it one cell each time

- Severity: low. Status: Reproduced once. Size: S.
- Where: `blueprint/orient.ts:43` to `47`.
- Scenario: `cannon-turret` flipped twice ends two cells to the left of where it started. Only coreless blueprints whose first part is a cannon, lance, laser, or bay are affected. 89 of the 90 stock blueprints pass.

## Bugs: CLI and validator

### T1. `validate` prints `ok` and exits 0 when a script file is missing

- Severity: high for the AI building loop. Status: Confirmed. Size: S.
- Where: `packages/cli/src/main.ts:181` to `182` prints a warning and carries on.
- Recipe: a blueprint with `"scripts": [{ "id": "drive", "source": { "file": "x.drive.js" }, "enabled": true }]` and no such file. `pnpm sim validate` prints `ok`, exit 0, with only `warning: script file x.drive.js not found next to the blueprint` on stderr. The robot then spawns with a script that stops at t=0.
- Change: make a missing script file an error for `validate`, `show`, `run`, and `duel`.

### T2. `validate` does not look inside scripts at all

- Severity: high. Status: Reproduced once. Size: M.

- Change: a check in the CLI, where QuickJS is already loaded, so `sim-core` stays pure: compile each script, require `tick`, and compare the string literals passed to `set(` and `get(` against the blueprint's tags, part types, and channel names. Report as warnings with the line number.

### T3. Misspelled flags are silently ignored

- Severity: medium. Status: Confirmed. Size: S.

- Recipe: `pnpm sim run car --secnds 1 --sead 9` runs the default 5 seconds on seed 1 and exits 0.
- Change: a table of allowed flags per command. An unknown flag exits 2 with a "did you mean" hint. Give `validate` and `show` a real `--json`.

### T4. `mirror --save` and `place --save` write the file even when the result does not validate

- Severity: low. Status: From reading. Size: S.
- Where: `main.ts:369` and `main.ts:437` write before checking the result.

### T5. Output streams and JSON shapes are inconsistent between commands

- Severity: low. Status: From reading. Size: S.

- Change: one rule. Data on stdout, problems on stderr, and under `--json` always a JSON object.

## Bugs: scripts and determinism

None of these change today's results on one machine. They are holes in "a replay is exact everywhere".

### D1. Three sorts in the simulation break ties with `localeCompare`

- Severity: low. Status: From reading. Size: S.
- Where: `physics/PhysicsWorld.ts:702`, `world/World.ts:1441`, `control/timeline.ts:21`.
- `localeCompare` depends on the machine's locale. When a ray meets two parts at exactly the same distance, the tie decides which part takes the shell.
- Change: compare with `<` and `>`, as `resources/pools.ts:73` already does.

### D2. A script at the recursion limit behaves differently depending on the host's stack

- Severity: medium. Status: From reading. Size: S to M.
- Where: `script/types.ts:22` to `26`, `script/quickjs.ts:277` to `285`.
- When QuickJS's own check fires first, the script gets an error it can catch and carry on. When the host's stack overflows first (a deeper call stack in the app, a smaller stack in another browser), the script is stopped. Same replay, different end.
- Change: make a stack overflow always fatal to the script, and add a test that calls a script from a deliberately deep host stack.

### D3. `async function tick` silently does nothing after its first `await`

- Severity: low to medium. Status: From reading. Size: S.
- Where: `script/quickjs.ts` never runs pending jobs, and the `Promise` intrinsic is on.
- Nothing crashes and nothing logs. Each tick queues a job that never runs, so memory climbs until the script is stopped for memory minutes later, which points the author at the wrong problem.
- Change: turn the `Promise` intrinsic off, or have the prelude reject a `tick` that returns a thenable. Add a validator warning for `async` and `await` in script source.

### D4. Native calls are outside the script budget

- Severity: medium for the app (a frozen tab), none for determinism. Status: From reading. Size: S to find out, S to M to fix.
- The budget is counted at bytecode poll points (`script/quickjs.ts:119` to `134`), and one long native call is one poll point. A backtracking regex or a huge `fill` or `sort` may never give the budget a chance to fire. `docs/design/04-control-and-scripting.md` lists `fill` as known. Regex is not listed.
- Change: test it first. If regex cannot be interrupted in this build, remove the `RegExp` intrinsic as `Date` already is, and lower the 16 MB memory limit.

### D5. Script error text is not capped, and is misfiled by its wording

- Severity: low. Status: From reading. Size: S.
- Where: `script/quickjs.ts:306` to `314`.
- An error whose message contains "stack overflow", "out of memory", or equals "interrupted" is reported as that kind whatever threw it, and loses its line number. The message has no length limit, unlike logs (300 characters).
- Change: apply the canned kinds only to QuickJS's own `InternalError`, and cut the message at 300 characters.

### D6. The world's seeded PRNG is dead code that is still hashed

- Severity: low. Status: From reading. Size: S.
- `this.rng` appears only at `World.ts:477` (created) and `World.ts:2710` (hashed). All randomness comes from the stateless `noise()` and the per-script seeded streams, which is the better design. `docs/design/01-architecture.md` still says "One seeded PRNG in World".
- Change: delete it (golden hashes move once) or fix the doc line.

## Quality of life

All of these are about the loop where an AI builds a robot from a sentence. Status: Reproduced once unless it says otherwise.

- **Q1. Errors should name what exists.** Size S each.
  - `BAD_CHANNEL: ... but wheel has no input 'throttle'` should add `(a wheel's inputs: speed)`. The list is one lookup away at `blueprint/validate.ts:314`.
  - `BAD_TARGET: ... targets 'guns' but no part has that tag or part type` should list the tags and part types present, with a "did you mean".
  - `BAD_CORE_CONTROLS`, `BAD_PRIMARY_CORE`, `BAD_CORE_PRIORITY` should list the cores present and say that ids are `<part>@<x>,<y>` counted from the bottom left, so adding a bottom row or a left column renames every part. This is the likeliest way to break a robot that has several cores.
- **Q2. Warn about grids that lie.** Size S each.
  - Rows of unequal length: dropping one token from a row usually still validates and spawns a lopsided robot. No stock blueprint has ragged rows, so a `RAGGED_GRID` warning costs nothing.
  - `=` is optional: `cannon-turret` with a `= =` row deleted still validates, and the cannon covers cells the grid shows as empty. Warn when a multi-cell part's other cells are not `=`.
- **Q3. Wrap the two commonest file mistakes.** Size S. A trailing comma gives a raw JSON error with no file name. A wrong path gives a raw `ENOENT`. An unknown blueprint name prints all 90 names: print the three nearest first.
- **Q4. Keys that can never fire pass validation.** Size S. `"key": "Hyper"`, `Shift`, `Ctrl`, `Up` all validate (`control/keys.ts:19`). So does a binding value of `1e308` on a channel whose range is 0 to 1.
- **Q5. A replay mismatch cannot say when or why.** Status: From reading. Size M. A replay holds one hash, at the end, and no version of anything (`replay/replayFile.ts:10` to `18`). Tuning one part's mass breaks every saved replay with the same message a real determinism bug gives. Add a hash of the part definitions, the Rapier version, and a checkpoint hash every 60 ticks, so `sim replay` can print "parts changed since this was saved" or "first difference between tick 540 and 600". The app already computes those hashes and throws them away.
- **Q6. Nobody can see how close a script runs to its budget.** Status: From reading. Size S. The meter already counts. Print the peak per script in `run` and `duel` ("peak 37 of 200 units in a tick"). Unlike milliseconds, the number is the same on every machine, so it could later back a counted tournament speed rule.
- **Q7. `sim determinism` compares two runs inside one process.** Status: From reading. Size S to M. Add `--fresh` to run the second in a child process, and print the first tick that differs.
- **Q8. Two coordinate systems in error messages.** Size S. `UNKNOWN_TOKEN` counts rows from the top. Every other message counts cells from the bottom. Print both.

## Checked and found fine

- **A loaded machine cannot change a match or break a replay.** The script budget counts interrupt callbacks and never reads a clock (`script/quickjs.ts:119` to `141`). Nothing in `sim-core` reads a clock, `Math.random`, or a browser global. The two `timedHost.ts` wrappers only measure. The app's 10 ms frame budget decides how many whole ticks run, never cuts one short.
- **Measured under heavy load:** `sim determinism` on a fabricator drone, a carrier, a cannon drone, a grapple drone, and a walker, each with two enemy drops over 1200 ticks, gave equal hashes every time while wall time per run grew from 40 to 75 s. No titan was checked.
- **Every other sort has a total order,** and every iterated Map or Set is filled in spawn or event order.
- **The sandbox is carefully built.** The prelude captures what it needs before user code runs, and the host re-validates every output (shape, finite numbers, counts, lengths) and clamps values to the channel's range. A script cannot get NaN, Infinity, or a string into a channel.
- **A crashed script leaves the robot limp, not stuck.** Script writes are cleared every tick, so channels fall back to their defaults. That is the right call.
- **All 90 stock blueprints validate** with no errors. The only warnings are `NO_CORE` on blueprints meant to be coreless.
- **Round trips hold for all 90:** serialize then parse, mirror twice, four quarter turns, and grid then expand all return the same blueprint.
- **About 250 fuzzed blueprints** (100 hand-written bad ones and 149 mutations of stock ones) produced none that validated and then threw at spawn or in its first second. That includes a 3600 part grid and coordinates of 1e300.
- **The structural validator messages are good:** they name the part, the cell, and often the fix.

## Performance (rough)

Measured while the machine was short of memory, so only the proportions mean anything. Re-measure before acting.

- In the hover, battle, and big-robot benches, scripts take about half of each tick and the rest of the world the other half. Both grow linearly with the number of robots.
- A CPU profile of a small battle: 39 percent in WASM (QuickJS and Rapier together, with the QuickJS interpreter loop the largest single function at 11 percent), 17 percent in `World.ts`, 5 percent in `TextDecoder` reading each script's result string out of QuickJS, 5 percent in Rapier's JS glue.
- Leads worth a look: the result string decode per script call, the contacts JSON built as text every tick (`script/frame.ts`), closures created per tick in `World.ts`, and `poolTotals` sorting a fresh array on every call.

## Ideas

One reviewer's proposals. All are "From reading". None was checked against existing plans beyond `docs/design/06-milestones.md` and `07-open-questions.md`.

**The main opinion:** a fourth weapon milestone would be a mistake. M13, M14, and M15 added three ways to kill on a flat, featureless line, and no new reason to move. The arena has no cover, 45 percent of titan matches are draws, and a whole class of parts has nothing to act on until the ground has a shape.

**What is already special and worth pushing:**

- The AI robot-building loop (validator, `show`, `run` with an ASCII side view, the playbook). 90 blueprints and eight generated titans came out of it. Next: a shared script library so one pilot is not kept as ten synced copies, and a `sim why <a> <b>` post-mortem of a duel.
- Parts are data, and it held for 36 parts. Several proposals below need no code.
- Everything that fools or feeds a robot goes through sensors. Strategies such as a jammer-lit decoy hull and a spotter feeding artillery by radio fell out of that rule without being built as features.
- Destruction is structural, and strategy followed it: half the titan generators are about where to put the core.
- The duel and tournament harness is a real test bench. It is the loop that would let an AI design a counter to the current champion between rounds.

## Not covered

- **The browser app.** Rendering, UI, storage, the builder's interaction, keyboard handling, and performance in the browser were not reviewed. E1 to E4 came from the independent pass, not from an app review.
- **Weapons beyond the charged guns.** Sensors, jamming, flares, guidance, target selection, the grapple, and the fabricator were not reached.
- **Balance beyond titan round 2.** No new matches were run, and no part-by-part table (damage per energy, health per mass, dominated parts) was produced.
- **Architecture, test gaps, and CI.** Only the rough bench numbers above exist. `World.ts` at 3088 lines was not mapped.
- **Hostile scripts.** D2 to D5 are from reading. A harness of hostile scripts (endless loops, huge allocations, deep recursion, prototype tampering, backtracking regex) was written and never run. It would be worth writing again as a test file.
- **Replays.** There is no replay fixture in the repository, so `sim replay` was not exercised.
- **Ideas for modes, worlds, and the next milestones** were planned and not written.

## Open questions for Logan

Each has a recommendation, so the answer can be "agree with all except #N".

1. **Charged gun recoil (W1).** Should a robot's own shot never count as a crash or trip its own fuzes, as the design doc says? Recommend yes. A light robot is still thrown by the recoil, which is punishment enough.
2. **A bolt's shove on its target (W1).** May it crash a light target on top of the bolt's damage? Recommend no. The doc calls bolt damage "no modifiers".
3. **Wins by out of bounds (Balance).** Half of all titan wins are a pilot walking its own core over the line. Recommend letting scripts read the arena's bounds and fixing the stock pilots, and keeping the rule itself.
4. **Draws at the time limit (Balance).** Give the win to the side with more than 20 points more of its parts left? Recommend yes. It would have decided 41 of round 2's 75 draws, and a wrecked robot would stop earning half a point.
5. **Wide bolt edges and cover (W2).** Should an edge that met terrain stay dead for the rest of the flight, so cover behind a berm is real? Recommend yes.
6. **Stack overflow in a script (D2).** Always fatal, so every host agrees? Recommend yes.
7. **`async` and promises in scripts (D3).** Turn the `Promise` intrinsic off? Recommend yes. Nothing in the simulation can await anything.
8. **Replay stamps (Q5).** Add the parts hash and 60 tick checkpoints? Recommend yes.
9. **Next milestone (Ideas).** Terrain with height and worlds that place blueprints, before another weapon? Recommend yes.
10. **Ammo as a resource (Ideas).** It would end "2000 guns firing for 240 s", and it would also end the fun of infinite ammo. Recommend deciding after terrain, with duel data.
11. **`World.rng` (D6).** Delete it and re-record the golden hashes? Recommend yes.
